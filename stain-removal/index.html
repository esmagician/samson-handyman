Service overview

Prepare the cause and surface before adding fresh paint

Some marks bleed back through normal emulsion, even after several coats. A better repair starts by confirming that any water source is fixed, cleaning the surface and choosing a suitable stain-blocking primer.

Depending on the existing finish and colour, the affected patch, full wall or ceiling section can then be repainted for a more even result.

Common marks

Suitable wall and ceiling stain work

Old water marks

Dry marks left after a roof, plumbing or bathroom leak has been repaired can be assessed, stain blocked and repainted.

Smoke and residue

Suitable painted surfaces with smoke or nicotine residue may need thorough cleaning and a specialist primer before decoration.

Scuffs and discolouration

Stubborn marks, rubbed areas and local discolouration can be prepared and blended or the full section repainted where that will look better.

Clear scope

Painted surfaces rather than specialist fabric cleaning

This service is for suitable decorated walls, ceilings and similar painted surfaces. It does not cover carpets, rugs, mattresses, upholstery, delicate wallpaper or hazardous contamination.

Active leaks, damp plaster or uncertain contamination must be resolved before decorative work begins. Extensive fire or smoke damage needs a specialist cleaning and restoration contractor.

Usually suitable

  • Local smoke or nicotine residue
  • Scuffs and stubborn discolouration

Not included

Before you book

Questions about wall and ceiling stains

Can an old water mark be painted over?

Only after the leak is repaired and the surface is fully dry. It may then need cleaning, stain-blocking primer and repainting.

Do you clean carpet or upholstery stains?

No. This service is for suitable painted walls, ceilings and similar decorated surfaces, not specialist fabric cleaning.

Can smoke or nicotine staining be treated?

Some painted surfaces can be cleaned, sealed with a suitable stain blocker and repainted. Photos and the extent are needed first.

Will the paint match exactly?

A patch may remain visible where old paint has aged. Repainting the full wall or ceiling section often gives a more consistent finish.
